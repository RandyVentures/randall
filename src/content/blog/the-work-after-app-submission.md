Submitting an app feels like the finish line.

It is not. It is the moment the work changes shape.

The latest stretch has included two new submissions—Heirloom and Fillbook—along with continued improvements to Rally Track, Bean Hunt, and Real Estate Manager. That makes this a useful point to step back and look at what happens after the code is written and the review button is pressed.

The next phase is about feedback.

## Submission Is a Milestone, Not a Conclusion

Getting an app submitted is a meaningful accomplishment, especially when you are building as a small team or as one person.

There are a lot of invisible steps before that moment: product decisions, UI work, testing, screenshots, metadata, privacy details, payments, support pages, and the final release checklist. It is easy to treat approval as the finish line because so much work has already gone into reaching it.

But submission only answers one question:

**Is the app ready to be reviewed?**

It does not answer the questions that matter next:

- Will people understand what the app does?
- Will they use it more than once?
- Where will they get stuck?
- Will they think it is worth rating?
- Which feature should be improved next?
- Is the app solving the problem clearly enough?

Those answers come from the next stage.

## Two New Ideas Waiting for Review

Heirloom and Fillbook are both waiting for App Store review, but they solve very different problems.

### Heirloom: Giving Old Letters a Second Life

Heirloom is an old letter reader designed to help people work with handwritten letters, records, diaries, and other documents that can be difficult to read.

Old documents often contain meaningful information, but the barrier is obvious: handwriting changes over time, pages fade, and the people who could once read them may not always be available to explain them.

Heirloom helps turn photographs of those documents into readable text. That makes the material easier to revisit, search, and understand.

The goal is not to replace the feeling of holding an original letter. It is to make the contents more accessible. A box of family documents should not become useful only to someone with enough time and patience to decode every page.

Heirloom is still waiting for review. Once it is public, the next question will be how people use it with their own documents. The most valuable feedback may come from the types of handwriting, languages, and documents that people bring to it.

### Fillbook: Making Trading History Easier to Review

Fillbook is a trading journal for options and futures traders.

Trading data can become complicated quickly. A single position may involve multiple fills, several legs, and changes across expiration dates. A raw export may contain the information, but it does not necessarily make the story of the trade easy to understand.

Fillbook is designed to organize that activity into a more useful review process. It can import broker exports, group fills into positions, follow rolls across expirations, and give traders a clearer place to examine what happened.

That distinction matters. A journal should not only preserve the past. It should help someone learn from it.

Fillbook also takes a local-first approach, keeping the journal centered on the trader's own device and workflow. The app does not require a broker login to begin reviewing imported activity.

Fillbook is also still waiting for review. The first public release will answer important questions: Which parts of the review process are most useful? What do traders expect to see first? Which information helps them make better decisions rather than simply creating another report?

## Ratings Are Part of the Product Experience

Rally Track and Bean Hunt are further along, but they are still in the same feedback loop.

Both apps have received work around ratings and user feedback. That may sound like marketing work, but it is also product work.

The rating flow still uses Apple's native rating prompt. The change is that it can appear sooner after meaningful events, when users have had a chance to get value from the app. The goal is to make it easier for satisfied customers to share their experience without turning the request into an interruption.

For Rally Track, that moment may come after a match or after the app has made scorekeeping easier. For Bean Hunt, it may come after someone finds a useful coffee shop or logs an experience they want to remember.

The important part is not asking everyone immediately. The important part is creating a natural path for satisfied users to share feedback.

Ratings help with discovery, but they also create a signal for the person building the app. A review can reveal confusion, a missing feature, or a use case that was not obvious during development.

## Real Estate Manager Keeps Expanding

Real Estate Manager is another example of why the work continues after launch.

The latest updates focus on lease tracking and move-in and move-out tracking, alongside the broader property-management workflow.

Property management is made up of recurring details: properties, units, tenants, leases, rent, payments, expenses, and dates that need attention later. Good features make those details easier to organize and easier to review when the information matters.

The best improvements in a property app are often not flashy. They reduce repeated work. They make an important detail easier to find. They turn something that used to live in a note, spreadsheet, or memory into something that can be checked in a few seconds.

That kind of progress compounds.

## The Real Product Loop

The portfolio is now moving through several stages at once:

- Heirloom and Fillbook are waiting for App Store review.
- Rally Track and Bean Hunt are learning from ratings and user feedback.
- Real Estate Manager is expanding its lease and move-tracking features.
- The next improvements will be shaped by actual use rather than assumptions.

That is the real product loop:

**Build, submit, observe, improve, repeat.**

No single release answers every question. A focused app becomes stronger through the small discoveries that happen after launch.

The goal is not to build endlessly. It is to keep improving the parts that create real value for the people using the app.

Submission is a milestone.

The feedback that follows is where the next version begins.
