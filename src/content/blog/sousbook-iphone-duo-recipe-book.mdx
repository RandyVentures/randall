A recipe can fit on a phone and still be awkward to cook from. You scroll up for the ingredients, scroll down for the directions, and forget whether that was one teaspoon or one tablespoon. Meanwhile, something is already sizzling.

That is the problem we are building **Sousbook** around. It is a recipe organizer for iPhone, and it is nearly complete. With iPhone Duo, the goal gets especially interesting: make the phone you already carry feel like a recipe book when you open it on the kitchen counter.

The extra space gives ingredients and directions room to sit beside each other. Your cookbook can stay visible while you look through a recipe. The screen becomes useful in a different way.

**The images below are real Sousbook captures from Apple's iPhone Duo simulator**, using a sample cookbook. They show development previews, not photographs of a physical Duo. The sample recipes illustrate the interface; a new personal cookbook starts empty. The app's cookbook and cooking features require Sousbook Pro after the sample introduction.

## Why iPhone Duo makes sense as a digital recipe book

A cookbook is a good example of information that benefits from having two things visible at once. When you are reading a novel, the next paragraph is enough. When you are cooking, you need the instruction and its quantities together.

Sousbook's expanded layout puts the recipe collection on the left and the selected recipe on the right. You can browse dinner ideas without repeatedly backing out of the recipe you just opened. Search, favorites, and categories help keep your own collection manageable.

## How we set up the iPhone Duo simulator in Xcode

We used Apple's simulator to build and test the layouts before having Duo hardware. Our local setup has **Xcode 27.1, build 27A9275**, and an **iOS 27.1 iPhone Duo simulator**. We keep that Xcode installation alongside our regular installation and select it explicitly for Duo work.

As of October 11, 2026, [Apple's iPhone Duo developer page](https://developer.apple.com/iphone-duo/) points developers to **Xcode 27.1 Release Candidate**. Apple's [system requirements](https://developer.apple.com/xcode/system-requirements) list macOS Tahoe 26.6 or later for that version. Xcode 27.2 beta 2 is also listed, but 27.1 RC is the Duo toolchain Apple currently highlights.

If you want to get the iPhone Duo simulator running, the setup is:

1. Get Xcode 27.1 RC from [Apple Developer downloads](https://developer.apple.com/download/all/) and install it on a supported Mac.
2. Open that copy of Xcode and go to **Xcode → Settings → Components**.
3. Install the required iOS platform support and iOS 27.1 simulator runtime. Wait for installation to finish.
4. Select an iPhone Duo simulator as the app's run destination and launch the app.
5. Exercise the expanded and compact displays, resizing, and folding transitions. A good screenshot in one position is only the beginning.

Apple documents component installation in [Downloading and installing additional Xcode components](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/downloading-and-installing-additional-xcode-components). If Duo is missing, check the selected Xcode version and installed components first.

## A personal cookbook from websites, photos, and your own recipes

Most of us already have recipes scattered across bookmarks, photos, printed pages, and messages. Sousbook brings those into one place through supported website imports, pasted text, manual entry, and camera or photo scans.

You review imported text before saving. Multi-photo scanning lets a recipe span more than one page. On supported devices where Apple Intelligence is available, optional scan cleanup offers changes for review, including joining directions that were split across lines. You decide what to apply.

Saved recipes are stored on the device and can be read offline. Website imports need an internet connection. Private iCloud sync is part of the app's design, with final production acceptance checks still in progress. There is no separate Sousbook account to create. More detail is in the [Sousbook privacy policy](/sousbook/privacy/).

One concrete lesson from Duo testing: an earlier layout change dismissed an unsaved photo-import draft when the phone unfolded. We changed how the import screen stays open when the layout changes, then tested a two-photo import through open → closed → open. The draft retained its pages, title, and quantities. That kind of continuity matters if this is going to be someone's everyday recipe book.

## Questions about Sousbook and iPhone Duo

### Can I use iPhone Duo as my primary recipe book?

That is the experience we are building toward: one personal cookbook for browsing, groceries, and cooking, with ingredients and steps side by side when there is enough space. The images here show the app running in the simulator.

### Can Sousbook scan printed recipes?

Yes. The current app supports camera and photo imports, including multiple photos, with a review step before saving. Check the resulting text and quantities against the original.

### Can I read saved recipes offline?

Yes. Saved recipes are stored locally. Importing a website, syncing through iCloud, and purchasing or restoring Pro require connectivity.

### Which Xcode version did we use for iPhone Duo?

Xcode 27.1 with the iOS 27.1 simulator runtime. At the time of writing, Apple recommends Xcode 27.1 RC on its Duo developer page; check its linked requirements and downloads for updates.
