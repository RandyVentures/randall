Someone you care about is running a race. You know when it starts, and you might know roughly when they expect to finish. In between, you’re wondering how it’s going.

Have they reached the halfway point? Are they getting close to the finish? Is there a way to send encouragement without expecting them to stop and answer a text?

That’s the experience I’m building [HypeLap](https://hypelap.com/) around: letting friends and family follow a runner’s race progress and take part by cheering them on.

**HypeLap is now live on the App Store for iPhone and Apple Watch.** The runner shares a link. Friends and family open it in their browser, watch the race progress, and send sound effects, messages, or voice notes that play in the runner’s earbuds.

You can be part of someone’s race even when you can’t be beside the course.

## One link brings the cheering section together

Race day already comes with enough coordination: getting to the start, finding parking, checking equipment, and making sure everyone knows the plan.

Following the runner should be straightforward.

With HypeLap, the runner selects a race and shares a watch link with the people they want cheering them on. They can send it directly to a friend, put it in the family group chat, or share it with their running crew.

Friends and family don’t need to download the app or create an account. They open the link in a browser and follow along.

That includes supporters using Android phones. HypeLap’s runner app is currently available for iPhone and Apple Watch, while the spectator experience works through the web.

Watching and cheering are free for supporters. The runner’s live tracking features are available through HypeLap’s paid runner options, including Pro or a single-race pass.

## Follow the race without asking for updates

Imagine your sister is running her first half marathon. You want to know how she’s doing, but you also know she has other things to concentrate on.

A message asking “Where are you now?” gives her another thing to respond to.

Opening her HypeLap link lets you follow her progress instead. The spectator page shows distance covered, elapsed time, and movement along a visual race track. If you open the link before she starts, you’ll see a waiting screen that updates when the race begins.

The display represents race progress. It doesn’t show the runner’s precise GPS location on a street map, so use it to follow the effort rather than decide which corner to stand on.

For family watching from home, that progress gives the race a little more shape. You can see the distance adding up and recognize when your runner is approaching the finish.

## Send encouragement they can hear

Following a race is one part of the experience. Being able to encourage the runner is the part I’m most excited about.

HypeLap lets supporters send cheers from the same page they’re watching. You can tap a sound effect, type a short message, or record a voice note.

An air horn or cowbell brings some race-day energy. A voice note makes it personal.

Think about hearing:

> “We’re all watching. You’ve got this!”

Or:

> “Proud of you. Keep going!”

Those are example messages, but they capture what I want this feature to make possible: a familiar voice arriving during the effort.

Cheers play through the runner’s audio, including their earbuds, and can play over music. The runner can mute or block a supporter if they want to control what they hear.

## Be there when you can’t make it to the race

Not everyone who cares about a runner can attend.

Parents may live in another city. A friend might be working. Someone may be home with the kids. Even people attending the race might only see their runner pass once or twice.

A shared race link gives those people a way to participate.

They can check progress during a break, send a quick cheer, or gather around a phone as the runner gets closer to finishing. They don’t have to learn a new app just to support someone for an afternoon.

That matters to me because the cheering section is often bigger than the group standing at the finish line. HypeLap gives more of those people a way to join in.

## Set up your cheering crew before race morning

A little preparation makes the experience easier for everyone.

For the runner:

1. Choose your race in HypeLap.
2. Share your watch link with friends and family.
3. Let them know when you expect to start.
4. Check your tracking and audio setup before race day.
5. Start your session when you’re ready to run.

For supporters:

1. Open the link your runner sent.
2. Leave the waiting page open or return around the start time.
3. Follow the race progress.
4. Send a cheer or a short voice note while they’re running.

Live updates and cheers depend on connectivity, so trying the setup together before the event is useful. It also gives everyone a chance to hear how the cheers work.

## It works beyond the biggest races

A race doesn’t need to offer its own tracking app for HypeLap to be useful. Tracking comes from the runner’s own device, so the race organizer doesn’t have to sign up.

Houston races are listed in the app, and runners can also paste a race signup link or create a group race.

That opens the experience to local races and running-club events as well as bigger occasions. A first 5K can mean just as much to someone’s family as a marathon. Having a few familiar people following along can make that effort feel shared.

## Your race, with your people alongside it

The runner still does the work. They put in the training, get to the start, and cover the distance.

What friends and family can offer is encouragement.

That’s what I want HypeLap to make easier: share a link, let your people follow your progress, and hear from them while you’re out there.
