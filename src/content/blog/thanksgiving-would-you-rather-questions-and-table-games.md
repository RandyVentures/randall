We're keeping this Thanksgiving week simple at our house: a couple of slow days at home, a lot of food, and a birthday party on Saturday. No big plans, which is exactly when a good table game helps. It gives everyone something to talk about besides work, school, and the weather.

Would you rather is the easiest one to start. Everyone can answer in two seconds, nobody has to share anything they don't want to, and the "wait, *why*?" that follows is where the good stories come out. Here are 70 Thanksgiving would you rather questions, sorted so you can start silly and get a little deeper by dessert, plus five easy table games that need nothing but a phone.

If you want straight conversation questions instead, I also put together [60 Thanksgiving questions for kids, adults and the whole table](/blog/thanksgiving-questions-for-kids-adults-and-the-whole-table/).

## How to play would you rather at Thanksgiving

- **One person reads the question.** Everyone answers at the same time by pointing left or right, so nobody just copies the last person.
- **Ask one "why" from each side.** That's the whole game. The answers are fine; the reasons are the fun part.
- **Pass the reading around.** Let the youngest kid and the oldest grandparent each take a turn.
- **Anyone can pass.** A question someone's happy to answer beats one everyone has to.
- **Five or six questions is plenty** during the meal. Save the rest for dessert or the drive home.

## 5 easy Thanksgiving table games (no supplies needed)

### 1. Would you rather, round robin

Use the questions above. Go around the table and let each person read one. Everyone answers at once, then one person from each side explains. It works for ages 5 to 95.

### 2. The gratitude round

A Thanksgiving gratitude game that doesn't feel like homework. Instead of "what are you thankful for?" (which gets you "family" eight times in a row), give everyone a specific prompt:

- Something you're thankful for that's smaller than your hand.
- Someone at this table and one thing they did this year.
- Something that was hard this year that you're glad happened anyway.

Specific prompts get real answers.

### 3. Thanksgiving two truths and a lie

Everyone shares three Thanksgiving memories, and one of them is made up. The table guesses which one is the lie. Grandparents tend to win this one, and it's a sneaky way to hear stories you've never heard.

### 4. The leftover draft

Like a fantasy football draft, but for leftovers. Go around in order and each person drafts one leftover for their ideal next-day plate. No duplicates. The arguments about whether stuffing beats pie in the first round are half the fun.

### 5. Draw a card

If the table wants to keep talking after the questions run out, pass a phone around and let each person draw a conversation card. That's the game I built.
