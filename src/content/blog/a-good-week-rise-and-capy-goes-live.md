It has been a good week.

Rise & Capy is now live, Heirloom received another annual subscription, I had a great time at a recent church greeters event, and I attended my third meeting of The Vanguard, a men’s group at church.

At work, I also placed second in an innovation challenge.

None of those things are the same kind of accomplishment, but together they made the week feel meaningful. There was progress in the products I am building, encouragement in the businesses behind them, connection through church, and a reminder that creative work can matter in my day job too.

## Rise & Capy Is Now Live

The biggest product milestone this week was seeing Rise & Capy go live.

There is always a difference between building an app and having an app available to the public. During development, the product exists mostly as a collection of screens, code, decisions, and tests. Once it is live, it becomes something another person can discover and use.

That transition is exciting because it makes the work more real. It also creates a new responsibility. The product now needs to be explained clearly, maintained, and improved based on what happens outside the development environment.

Getting Rise & Capy to this point required moving through the usual steps: building the experience, preparing the release, checking the details, and waiting for review. Those steps can feel slow while they are happening, but they are part of what turns an idea into a real product.

Now the next phase begins.

The app can gather feedback, create new questions, and show which parts of the original idea are useful to people. Launching is an accomplishment, but it is also the beginning of the product’s public life.

## Another Annual Subscription for Heirloom

Heirloom also had a good week.

Another person subscribed annually, which is encouraging because it shows that the app is becoming more than a project I built for myself. Someone saw enough value in the experience to make a longer-term commitment.

Small subscription milestones can be easy to overlook when compared with much larger software businesses. For an independent app, though, each subscriber represents a real person making a real decision.

That decision is meaningful.

An annual subscription says that the user expects the app to remain useful over time. It also creates a responsibility to keep the product dependable and continue improving it thoughtfully.

The goal is not to add features simply because a subscription exists. The goal is to take care of the product, listen to the people using it, and make improvements that strengthen the reason they subscribed in the first place.

Heirloom is still early, but these moments make the business feel more tangible. A project becomes a product when people choose to use it. It becomes a business when some of those people choose to support it.

## A Good Time With the Church Greeters

Outside of work and apps, I had a good time at a recent greeters event from church.

Serving as a greeter is a simple way to help create a welcoming environment. It is also a good opportunity to spend time with people I might not otherwise see during a normal week.

Events like that are valuable because they create connection without needing a complicated agenda. You get to talk, laugh, learn more about people, and enjoy being part of something larger than your own schedule.

It is easy to let work and projects become the center of attention because they always produce another task. Church events provide a different kind of rhythm. They remind me that showing up for people is productive in a way that does not always appear on a checklist.

I left the event grateful for the people who make church feel like a community rather than just a place to attend.

## The Vanguard

I also attended my third meeting of The Vanguard, a men’s group from church.

It has been going well, and I have appreciated having a regular place to talk about faith, family, work, responsibility, and personal growth with other men who are trying to take those things seriously.

Three meetings is still early, but consistency matters. A group becomes more meaningful when you continue showing up, learn people’s stories, and become willing to participate honestly instead of only listening from a distance.

The Vanguard has given me another space to slow down and reflect. Building apps and working toward financial freedom are important goals, but they are not the whole picture. The kind of husband, father, friend, and man I become while pursuing those goals matters just as much.

That is one reason groups like this are helpful. They keep growth connected to character.

## Second Place in an Innovation Challenge

The other major highlight was placing second in an innovation challenge at work.

Innovation challenges are fun because they create room to think differently from the normal routine. Instead of only focusing on the work already assigned, you get to explore an idea, shape it, and explain why it could be useful.

Placing second was an encouraging result. It was also a reminder that the instincts I use while building apps—looking for friction, imagining a better workflow, and turning an idea into something concrete—can be useful in my professional work too.

The challenge gave me a chance to be creative in a different environment. It also felt good to have that effort recognized by the people around me.

There is a connection between independent app building and innovation at work. Both require noticing a problem, making a thoughtful proposal, communicating clearly, and accepting that the first version may need to change.

## A Week With Different Kinds of Progress

Looking back, this week was not defined by one huge event.

It was a collection of smaller wins that touched different parts of life. Rise & Capy went live. Heirloom gained another annual subscriber. I spent time with people at church, attended another Vanguard meeting, and placed second in an innovation challenge at work.

Those wins point in different directions, but they also fit together.

The apps represent persistence and creative work. The subscription represents growing trust. The church events represent community and service. The work challenge represents curiosity and the willingness to contribute a new idea.

It is worth noticing all of them.

There will always be another feature to build, another task to finish, and another goal to chase. But a good life is also made up of the weeks when several important things quietly move forward at the same time.

This was one of those weeks, and I am grateful for it.
