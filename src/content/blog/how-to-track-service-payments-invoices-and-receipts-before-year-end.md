The holiday rush can be good for a service business—and hard on its paperwork.

One customer books Halloween decorations. Another needs a deep clean before Thanksgiving guests arrive. By December, you're installing Christmas lights, squeezing in extra jobs, and answering messages between appointments.

Then you sit down to review the year. Who paid? Which invoice is still outstanding? What was that payment for? Where's the receipt?

If the answers are scattered across texts, emails, and handwritten notes, every finished job becomes another record to reconstruct.

Here's a simple routine for keeping service payments, invoices, and receipts organized before year-end. The holiday examples below are illustrative, not customer stories.

## Give every service a clear description

A payment record is more useful when you can tell what it covered.

“Holiday work” might make sense today. A few months later, it could mean decorating, cleaning, installation, or removal.

Use descriptions that identify the service and when you performed it:

- **Halloween:** Front-porch decoration setup — October 24.
- **Thanksgiving:** Kitchen and guest-room deep clean — November 23.
- **Christmas:** Roofline light installation — December 2.
- **Year-round:** Lawn mowing and leaf cleanup — October 16.

For a job with several parts, use an itemized invoice. Separate lines make it easier for the customer to understand the bill and for you to recognize the work later.

A Christmas lighting invoice, for example, could list installation and a separately agreed takedown service. Make clear whether removal is included in the current bill or will be billed later.

## Send the invoice while the job is fresh

It's easy to finish a job, head to the next appointment, and tell yourself you'll send the invoice tonight.

During a busy week, tonight can turn into next Tuesday.

Make invoicing part of closing out the job:

1. Confirm the completed work and agreed amount.
2. Create the invoice or payment request.
3. Send the customer the link.
4. Check its payment status afterward.

With Mandalo, customers can open the link and pay through secure Stripe checkout without creating a Mandalo account. An itemized invoice includes a **Pay now** button, and the customer can print or save the invoice as a PDF.

If you enter an email address, Mandalo can email the link. To send it by text, copy the link into your message.

For a Thanksgiving cleaning job, that message could be:

> Hi [Name], your kitchen and guest-room cleaning is complete. Here's the invoice for the agreed work: [invoice link]. Payment is due [date]. Thank you, and enjoy the holiday!

The customer gets a clear next step while the details are easy to remember.

## Check whether the customer actually paid

Sending an invoice and receiving payment are separate steps.

An invoice tells the customer what they owe. A receipt records a payment. Keeping that distinction clear helps you avoid mistaking an unpaid job for money already received.

Mandalo's dashboard shows payment status so you can see which requests are paid and which are still pending. Paid invoices also provide a link to the receipt.

That helps answer the question that often gets buried in a text thread: “Did this customer pay me yet?”

For a Christmas light installer managing several homes, checking the dashboard is easier than rereading every conversation to see who said they would pay. A promise to pay is still something to follow up on until the payment arrives.

## Keep deposits and remaining balances clearly labeled

Some holiday jobs involve a payment before the work begins and another afterward.

If that is your agreed arrangement, make each request specific:

- Christmas light installation — booking deposit.
- Christmas light installation — remaining balance.

Include a consistent job reference in both descriptions so you can tell which requests belong together.

Mandalo currently handles this through two separate payment requests. It does not automatically split an invoice into installments or calculate the remaining balance for you.

Keep a record of the agreed total and each payment amount. The same approach can help distinguish Halloween decoration setup from a later removal visit, or separate services booked by a repeat customer.

## Review your records before the year gets away from you

Choose a regular time to review your payment activity. A short weekly check gives you a chance to catch missing details while you still remember the job.

Look for:

- Completed work you haven't invoiced yet.
- Pending payments that need a follow-up.
- Descriptions that need more detail.
- Separate requests that belong to the same job.
- Invoice and receipt records you need to retain for your bookkeeping.

For an unpaid invoice, keep the reminder straightforward:

> Hi [Name], I'm following up on the invoice for [service] completed on [date]. It's still showing as unpaid. Here's the link again: [invoice link]. If anything needs clarification, please let me know.

A lawn-care visit, pool cleaning, handyman repair, or house-cleaning appointment benefits from the same routine: describe the work, send the invoice, check payment status, and keep the receipt associated with the payment.
