If you have a friend or family member running a race, you probably want two things. You want to see how they're doing, and you'd like them to know you're thinking of them while they run.

A few different tools do part of that. They were built for different jobs, so the right one depends on what you need. Here is how **RaceJoy**, **Strava Beacon** and **HypeLap** compare for the people on the sidelines.

## Three questions that decide it

Before comparing apps, it helps to ask what you actually need:

1. **Can I follow them without installing anything?** Some tools need a spectator account or app, and some work from a link.
2. **Can I send something they will actually hear?** A runner in earbuds can't hear a text notification buzzing on their wrist the way they can hear a voice.
3. **Does it work at this race?** Some tools depend on the race organizer, and some work at any race.

## RaceJoy

RaceJoy is a race-day tracking app that many events offer to their participants. Its own site says spectators can follow participants live and send cheers and messages, and describes GPS tracking from the runner's phone. The race director section is about offering RaceJoy at an event, so whether you can use it depends on whether your race does.

What I couldn't confirm from RaceJoy's official pages is whether spectators need the app to follow along, and what it costs. Some race pages mention a small fee, but those pages can be out of date. The practical step is to look at your race's own RaceJoy page, which will tell you what your event offers and what it costs.

## Strava Beacon

Strava Beacon is a safety feature. You turn it on from the record screen in the Strava phone app, choose up to three contacts, or copy a link and send it yourself. Your contacts get a link that opens in a mobile browser, and they don't need a Strava account. The page updates about every 15 seconds, and it needs cell service.

Beacon is free when you record with the phone app. Strava says recording with a connected device, such as an Apple Watch or Garmin, is part of its subscription. Sharing stops when you finish the recording.

It's a good answer to "I want someone to know where I am on a training run." Beacon is designed for safety, not for cheering. I didn't find a way for the people watching to send audio cheers to the runner.

## Garmin LiveTrack, briefly

If your runner wears a Garmin, LiveTrack is also worth knowing about. It's free with the Garmin Connect app, and it gives your contacts a link to a page that shows position, time, distance and speed. Garmin's manual says a LiveTrack session ends on its own after 24 hours by default. I couldn't confirm from the sources I read whether people opening the link need an account, so check that with a test link.

## HypeLap

HypeLap is built for race day and for friends watching. Here is what it does, and what it doesn't.

- **Spectators:** friends and family open the runner's link in any phone browser. No app and no account, and watching and cheering are free.
- **What they see:** rank, pace and progress along a visual track. It deliberately does not show a map of where the runner is. That's a privacy choice: friends can follow how the race is going without seeing exactly where the runner is.
- **Cheers:** supporters can send an air horn, a cowbell, a text message or a voice note up to 15 seconds long. The sound plays in the runner's earbuds over their music, and the runner can mute or block anyone.
- **Any race:** it uses the runner's own iPhone or Apple Watch, so it doesn't depend on the race offering it.

The trade-offs are real. The runner's app is on iPhone and Apple Watch for now, with Android listed as coming soon. The runner pays for live tracking (Runner Pro is $39.99 a year, and there are race-by-race options), while spectators pay nothing. And because there is no map, a spectator can't use it to work out which street corner to stand on.

## So which one should you use?

- **Your race offers RaceJoy:** look at the race's tracking page and use it. It's the official option for that event.
- **You want a safety link on training runs:** Strava Beacon (or Garmin LiveTrack if you wear a Garmin).
- **You want friends to watch free and be heard in the runner's ears, at any race:** that's what I built HypeLap for.

They aren't mutually exclusive. A runner can use Beacon on a long training run and share a HypeLap link on race day.

## Before race day

Whatever you choose, test it ahead of time. Send the link to a friend on an Android phone and see what they see, and check that the runner can hear the cheers over their music at a volume that suits them. Race-day logistics are no time to discover a setting.
